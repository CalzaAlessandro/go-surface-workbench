How we will use them to calibrate charges

  1. Curate graph, units, groups, edges/H, protonation, total charge, and multiplicity; check duplicates and periodic cells.
  2. Select by size, oxidation, groups, clustering, and curvature. Preserve controlled fragments to separate distance and compositional effects.
  3. Separate training and test sets by sheet/parent and similar structures before fitting. Reserve external families never used for model selection.
  4. Recalculate electrostatic reference with a uniform protocol. Do not mix DFTB, DDEC, OPLS, and RESP charges as if they were the same label.
  5. Compare fixed library, context corrections, and SQE on the same target; enforce chemical total Q, without arbitrary redistributions onto basal carbon.

The AMBER constraint remains decisive

Following RESP does not mean that every fit to any QM density is automatically compatible with GAFF. The conventional HF/6-31G* target and AM1-BCC are documented by the official AMBER tutorial. Compatibility of the engine with a charge file does not demonstrate physical balance with proteins, ligands, water, ions, and LJ terms.

The choice therefore involves two steps: validate the electronic reference on QM properties (potential, dipoles, basis set, electronic state), then validate the fixed-charge model with the planned force field. Any alternative DFT/RESP target remains experimental until it passes both. Agreement among functionals alone is not sufficient; correlated controls are limited to molecules and require evaluation of multireference character. No missing T1 diagnostic is interpreted as a low T1.

Still to be decided: alternative QM reference, definitive SQE/context calibration, and choice of production charges. The launched jobs do not already constitute this decision.
