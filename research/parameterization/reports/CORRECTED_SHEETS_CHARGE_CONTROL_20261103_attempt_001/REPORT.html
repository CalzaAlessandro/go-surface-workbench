Before charges: the electronic graph requires verification

Amber stores bonds, but not a complete Lewis graph with all bond orders. I retained the library groups, one COO⁻ per CBD and formally neutral carbons, seeking the largest possible number of C=C double bonds compatible with valences.

At least 63 incomplete carbon sites in GOX and 28 in REGOX remain under these assumptions. The result is verified both by integer optimization and by an independent bipartite matching algorithm. GOX also has an odd number of electrons: with the current formula and charge, it cannot be an entirely closed-shell system.

These counts do not determine the physical number of unpaired spins, the multiplicity or the number of hydrogens to add. A different electronic description may include delocalization, charge separation or open-shell states. For the predictions I used two maximal completions, A and B, making the remaining sites explicit in the diagnostic graph. No passivation or chemical correction was applied to the originals.

Basal carbons: a near-zero average can hide broad distributions

With Espaloma, the average GGG carbon is −0.004 e in GOX and −0.040 e in REGOX, but approximately 56% and 55% of sites exceed |q| = 0.1 e. The A ranges are −0.414…+0.358 e and −0.380…+0.539 e, respectively. SQE produces narrower distributions. Since the electronic graph is not validated, these data are insufficient to refute your LIB's zero-basal-carbon approximation: they show where to compare well-defined QM references.

The average charge of the entire CBD residue, including the anchor carbon, is approximately −0.717/−0.430 e with Espaloma, −1.027/−1.044 e with SQE+q0 and −0.106/−0.110 e with SQE+qp (GOX/REGOX). The remaining charge is distributed over the sheet. Conservation of −16 e or −6 e alone does not guarantee physically correct localization.

Crucial check: carboxylate resonance alone

By swapping only O=C–O⁻ with ⁻O–C=O, with the same atoms, coordinates and total charge, SQE+q0 changes by up to approximately 0.65 e on one oxygen; SQE+qp by up to 0.24 e; Espaloma by up to 0.043 e. Variant C does not move the remaining carbon sites: it therefore separates this effect from the A/B ambiguity in the rest of the sheet. SQE+q0 also assigns the two oxygens values close to −1.23 e and −0.58 e, despite an average of approximately −0.90 e.

This check makes Espaloma more interesting than the current SQE sets for robustness to COO⁻ notation, without proving physical accuracy. Future calibration needs to treat symmetry and resonance explicitly: consistent typing, possible averaging over validated representations and comparison with RESP, including tests on whole molecules excluded from fitting.

Timings, methods and checks

Individual primary inferences required approximately 0.08–0.46 s for Espaloma and 0.10–0.18 s for SQE, on CPU. The cumulative process peak is approximately 290 MiB for Espaloma and 75 MiB for SQE. These timings exclude installation, preparation and weight loading; they do not demonstrate linear scaling to arbitrarily large sheets.

Espaloma v0.0.8 uses the unchanged published model and the explicit formal total in the q_ref field; the maximum total-charge error in these tests is approximately 5.1 × 10⁻⁶ e. SQE uses ChargeFW 0.1.7 natively, with CCD_gen sets from Schindler 2021, calibrated on NPA, not our RESP data. Strict matching, complete solution and no refitted parameters. SQE totals are conserved within approximately 1.5 × 10⁻¹² e. Atom permutation and, for SQE, rotation/translation of coordinates pass the numerical checks. Numerical reproducibility does not resolve the chemical ambiguity of the graph.

Is direct QM on the whole sheet possible?

Size alone does not make it impossible. CP2K/Quickstep is also designed for systems of approximately a thousand atoms. The Supporting Information for the GAFF2–AIM model describes single-point DFT on GO with more than 10000 atoms using PBE, a DZVP-MOLOPT-SR-GTH basis and reduced-scaling methods; however, it also states a limit of 25 SCF cycles and approximate energy convergence. That protocol produces DDEC6 charges and atomic volumes, not RESP HF/6-31G*.

For our objective, I would start by checking the electronic state and performing a well-controlled DFT single point on a selected sheet, to validate charge distribution and long-range behavior. The cost of a full HF/6-31G* calculation, geometry and multi-orientation RESP grids requires a separate benchmark before estimating whether the five-day queue limit is sufficient. Fragment RESP charges remain the Amber-consistent reference for training a reproducible, scalable charge assigner. QM on sheets with a charge of −16/−6 also requires defining the environment, counterions or embedding, and boundary conditions: an isolated gas-phase calculation and a surface in solution address different problems. The stability of isolated polyanions has not been estimated.
