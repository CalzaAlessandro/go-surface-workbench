AmberGO is a hybrid diagnostic comparison: its basal groups are the original ones, but terminal C/H charges are ±0.115, ±0.119646 or ±0.150 e. The ranges describe these three choices, not statistical uncertainty. This is not a test of the complete AmberGO force field.

What we can conclude

SQE is closest to the individual HF–RESP values among the transferable charge assigners evaluated. L0 better reproduces the HF potential, while SQE is slightly better against the current DFT reference. AmberGO is promising for OH/epoxides, but edge sensitivity prevents an overall ranking of the preset. The distance between charge vectors is not identical to potential quality: different internal charges can compensate for one another.

Molecular RESP fits are controls fitted to each individual system, so they are not an independent test of library transferability. The molecular_HF_RESP model has a median HF RRMS of 7.55%; molecular_DFT_RESP has a median DFT RRMS of 10.98%. These charges are not already available for an arbitrary sheet without a new calculation/fit.

GAFF2-AIM and OLD lack a validated mapping onto the same fragments, so they receive no numerical ranking. Raw GOPY has block-charge balance problems and is excluded from the main comparison. Native AM1-BCC has parameter coverage for only five of the seven contexts.

Additional GAFF2 fileGO_gaff2_Merve.frcmod is included, but the final MOL2 files follow simplified GAFF typing. Many terms in the GAFF2 file are assigned by analogy; the presence of the file does not establish a new, fully optimized parameterization.

ML dedicated to charges

EspalomaCharge and OpenFF NAGL exist for AM1-BCC-type charges. Espaloma has already been tested here, with inadequate performance on this GO subset: this does not mean it generally fails on molecules within its domain. NAGL has not yet been tested on our sheets. A predictor trained on RESP is possible, but requires RESP data and transferability checks; it cannot be obtained from standard GO-MACE without changing the model and target.

GO-MACE is left out of the charge-assignment workflow. No production parameters have been changed. New comparisons between QM levels remain in progress.
