Which reference is scientifically defensible?

Recommendation: use direct QM electronic properties as primary reference, verifying the DFT level; then separately validate the fixed-charge model in water. RESP is a fitting method and can use a DFT potential. The HF/6-31G* level is the conventional reference of the Amber branch, not a universal truth for GO.

The current control ωB97X-D/6-311++G(d,p) is a working reference: we have not yet proven that it is the best for GO. The next gate is to verify sensitivity to functional/basis set, electronic state, and size. Study on QM method dependence.

To derive effective fixed charges in water, RESP2 is a precedent for using gas/water references together with LJ validation. It is a direction to test for GO, not a protocol already adopted here. After assignment, charges remain constant in MD and GBSA. Protocol and missing verifications.

The RESP/BCC 50:50 blend does not improve on molecular RESP in this DFT control. In the five C24s with available BCC, it is worse than RESP and better than BCC in all five cases. This is a result on this panel, not a general law on charge models.

The reference is the electronic potential

We evaluate Gaussian HF/6-31G* and ωB97X-D/6-311++G(d,p) grids directly, not the potential reconstructed from their RESP charges. 54 ESP files and corresponding logs are verified: hash, method, convergence, total charge/spin, atomic identity, and identical geometry within coordinate precision.

Verification of logs confirms the stated method and multiplicity, not the ground state. HF references are conventional RHF controls; this post-analysis does not resolve already documented external instabilities or establish a new electronic stability.

DFT is an electronic check distinct from the HF–RESP target, but remains approximate. It is neither an exact truth nor an automatic validation in water. Atomic charges are not uniquely determined by QM: potential, density, and moments are more direct references than individual numbers from a partition.

The large residuals of Espaloma in these geometries suggest caution when transferring to GO.

Blending targets is a new model choice

Two separate calibrations, SQE–RESP and SQE–BCC, are consistent and comparable. A fit with both targets, or an average of the charges, produces a third compromise. The average preserves the total if both inputs have the same Q; the weighting must nevertheless be justified and validated against independent data. Here 50:50 is fixed a priori and purely diagnostic; no optimal weight was chosen after viewing test results.

AM1-BCC is parameterized to emulate the HF/6-31G* potential, so its agreement with RESP is not an independent confirmation of that reference. We do not claim that every BCC charge coincides with a particular RESP fit. Jakalian et al.

How much we can conclude

The control does not indicate that RESP is inherently flawed for GO. It shows that its molecular representation maintains good agreement with this second QM potential in the contexts studied. The picture remains limited by choice of functional/basis set, spin/stability, and finite fragments. For carboxyls, the two C16 examples are training diagnostics and do not enter the C24 median; in COO−, the monopole term can also make the relative RRMS small without guaranteeing every local detail.

Gas-phase QM tests electronic fidelity. Fixed charges for water are effective parameters: the known HF/6-31G* overpolarization can contribute to condensed-phase behavior. A model with better vacuum ESP must still be verified with consistent LJ and solvent, probe interactions, and independent observables. Discussion of QM reference and polarization.
