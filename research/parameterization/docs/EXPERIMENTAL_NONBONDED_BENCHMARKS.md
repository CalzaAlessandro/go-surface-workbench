# Experimental benchmarks for GO nonbonded parameters

**Literature check:** 2026-10-30. This note collects measured observables that can help compare new, coupled charge/Lennard-Jones (LJ) candidates for graphene oxide (GO). It is a benchmark map, not a parameter prescription. The existing published/current presets remain reference controls; this review does not alter them.

## Recommended benchmark set

| Observable | Primary study and reported measurement | What it can test | Material and interpretation limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Amino-acid adsorption thermodynamics | Pandit & De, “Interaction of Amino Acids and Graphene Oxide: Trends in Thermodynamic Properties,” *J. Phys. Chem. C* **121**, 600–608 (2017), [doi:10.1021/acs.jpcc.6b11571](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jpcc.6b11571). Isothermal titration calorimetry (ITC) at 25 °C in 5 mM sodium phosphate, pH 7.4; 20 mM amino acid titrant. Reported GO association constants include Arg 2.36 × 10³ M⁻¹ and Trp 4.55 × 10³ M⁻¹; corresponding reported −ΔG values are 19.24 and 20.87 kJ/mol. The study reports strongest binding for cationic and aromatic amino acids; Asp showed no detectable binding by ITC. | A useful cross-chemistry check on combined electrostatics, dispersion, and hydration: charged Arg/Lys/His versus aromatic Phe/Tyr/Trp, plus weak/nonbinding controls. The measured rank and thermodynamics can test the full interaction model. | The authors deliberately compared two GO size/oxidation preparations (“GO” and “nano-GO”); this is not a single universal GO surface. pH and buffer are essential because amino-acid and GO groups titrate. The tabulated association constants/ΔG are macroscopic, site-model-dependent observables, not direct atom-pair energies. Do not fit a single LJ or charge term to a single amino acid. |
| GO acid-base response and apparent surface charge | Konkena & Vasudevan, “Understanding Aqueous Dispersibility of Graphene Oxide and Reduced Graphene Oxide through pKa Measurements,” *J. Phys. Chem. Lett.* **3**, 867–872 (2012), [doi:10.1021/jz300236w](https://doi.org/10.1021/jz300236w). Potentiometric pH titration, zeta potential, and IR gave apparent GO ionization pK values of 4.3, 6.6, and 9.0; the authors assign much of charge buildup to carboxyl ionization, with nearby phenolic/hydroxyl groups shifting acidity. | A chemistry/protonation check before comparing charge-sensitive adsorption or salt response. A model with fixed protonation should be compared only to a matching pH/protonation state; a pH-responsive model can be checked against the titration curve and ionized-site trend. | These are apparent, sample-specific group populations and pK values, not a universal atom-charge assignment. Zeta potential is an electrokinetic potential at the slipping plane; it is not a measurement of partial atomic charges or a direct surface-charge density. Do not set atom charges equal to ζ-derived values. |
| Liquid-water swelling / salt screening | Zheng et al., “Swelling of Graphene Oxide Membranes in Aqueous Solution: Characterization of Interlayer Spacing and Insight into Water Transport Mechanisms,” *ACS Nano* **11**, 6440–6450 (2017), [doi:10.1021/acsnano.7b02999](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsnano.7b02999). Using an integrated QCM-D/ellipsometry method, a dry GO membrane initially had d-spacing 0.76 nm after immersion; it later reached 6–7 nm in water. In NaCl and Na₂SO₄, spacing decreased as ionic strength rose, reaching about 2 nm at 100 mM. | Collective water uptake, interlayer cohesion, and the response to electrolyte screening. Compare the direction and concentration dependence, and distinguish Na⁺/Cl⁻ from sulfate effects when constructing a model test. | This is a stacked membrane observable with evolving galleries and long equilibration, not a single-sheet hydration free energy. A finite periodic bilayer cannot be expected to reproduce the macroscopic 6–7 nm equilibrium spacing without modeling the relevant swelling/stacking regime. The paper’s reported salt dependence is not a standalone calibration of atomic charges. |
| Water-vapor sorption and humidity-dependent spacing | Lian et al., “Extraordinary water adsorption characteristics of graphene oxide,” *Chem. Sci.* **9**, 5106–5111 (2018), [doi:10.1039/C8SC00545A](https://doi.org/10.1039/C8SC00545A). For vacuum-filtered GO laminates, the reported water uptake reached 0.58 g water/g GO at relative pressure P/P₀ = 0.9. XRD spacing changed from 6.5 Å when dry to 11.3 Å at P/P₀ = 0.9. Isotherms were measured at 25 and 40 °C; the paper reports an isosteric water-adsorption enthalpy about 30% above silica gel. | Coupled water–GO and water–water attraction across humidity, including whether a model captures low-pressure uptake versus higher-pressure multilayer/capillary filling. The spacing and uptake together are more informative than either alone. | This is a laminated, porous membrane with wrinkles and multiple adsorption regimes; uptake/enthalpy include gallery filling and morphology, not just local GO–water LJ contacts. Enthalpy is inferred from temperature-dependent isotherms (Clausius–Clapeyron), not a direct pair potential. Match laminate preparation, drying and relative pressure before quantitative comparison. |

## How to use these data in the candidate comparison

1. **Keep the GO chemistry explicit.** Record oxidation level/C:O, functional-group proportions and arrangement, lateral size, edge density, preparation route, and whether the sample is powder, flake, or laminate. Hummers-derived and other GO samples, reduced GO, and pristine graphene are not interchangeable surfaces.
2. **Match solution state.** Record temperature, pH, buffer identity/concentration, salt species and ionic strength, and amino-acid protonation. In particular, comparison to the Pandit–De ITC values requires the 25 °C, pH 7.4 phosphate-buffer condition and the relevant amino-acid charge state.
3. **Use measured macroscopic values as held-out or multi-observable checks.** Evaluate rank/order and condition dependence for adsorption, and uptake plus spacing for hydration. These observables constrain the total nonbonded model (charges + LJ + water/ions + surface chemistry); none identifies a unique parameter by itself.
4. **Separate measurement from derived quantities.** In the Pinto et al. SI, the amino-acid reference free energies are compiled from experiments and converted for LIE comparisons. For one data set, the authors state that the reported information did not provide the GO binding-site molecular weight needed to calculate concentration of sites, so they estimated that quantity by fitting to the other set. Treat those converted/reference ΔG values as model-dependent derived benchmarks, not independent direct measurements. The supplied local SI is `references/papers/Pinto2023_SI.txt`.
5. **Keep ζ potential in its proper role.** It is useful as a condition-matched colloidal/electrokinetic response, especially across pH or salt series, but it is not a per-atom charge target. Fixed-charge candidates should first use an explicit, stated protonation model and then be tested against solution observables.

## Provenance and limits

Primary-paper pages/abstracts and accessible article text or supporting information were checked for the listed quantities. Search date is 2026-10-30. Exact lateral dimensions, oxidation metrics, and all buffer/salt details were not independently recovered for every experiment; where not stated above, they remain to be checked in the full paper/SI before building a strict numerical fitting set. No published preset or active force-field file was edited for this literature review.
