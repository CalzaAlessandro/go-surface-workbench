# GO functional groups outside the present library

The nine indexed units are GGG, GRT, HYD, EPX, EPH, EPT, CAB, CBX and CBD: scaffold/caps, basal alcohol, three epoxide substitution patterns, carbonyl, COOH and COO−. They are a defined working subset. EPH/EPT remain epoxide variants; they do not provide general ether coverage. This review identifies expansion candidates, not new populations, parameters or claims that every GO sample contains every motif.

**Lactone and lactol require different connectivity.** A lactone contains a cyclic ester C(=O)–O linkage; a lactol contains a cyclic hemiacetal center bearing OH and OR. A complex structure may contain both features, but the names are not interchangeable. A signal assigned to a hemiacetal carbon does not by itself identify an ester carbonyl.

## How strong is the lactone case?

Original GO-specific evidence exists in Aliyev et al.'s study. Boehm's “lactonic” category remains operational; the approximately 100 ppm lactone-with-OH/lactol terminology does not resolve both graphs. Treat the proposed structure as a testable interpretation, rather than a ring-resolved inventory. [A19, printed pp. 7–9 and 12–13]

Thomas et al. discuss an unresolved ester/lactone/acid carbonyl envelope in **extracted oxidation debris**. This supports distinguishing sheet chemistry from associated soluble material; it does not justify transferring that envelope into a fixed density of lactones on an intact sheet. [T13, printed p. 3584] No generic carbon-black/biochar motif list is used as proof of GO composition here.

## Interpretation and expansion priorities

**Inference for model selection:** overlapping assignments in the inspected studies mean that XPS C–O and O–C=O envelopes alone cannot select unique ether/epoxide/alcohol or acid/ester ring topologies. Require complementary structural/chemical evidence, preserve synthesis/annealing/base-washing/water history, and distinguish reported assignments from proposed mechanisms. Do not derive new group fractions solely from a fitted XPS component.

My suggested order is: **(1)** non-epoxide ethers for aged/rGO and phenolic OH where relevant; **(2)** explicit lactol and lactone/ester candidates with separate evidence labels, plus broader quinone contexts; **(3)** gem-diols and anhydrides as conditional hypotheses. Organosulfate moves up the list if target-material sulfur analysis and synthesis history support it. These priorities are a modeling recommendation, not measured abundance.

The archived [HierGO README](https://github.com/IFM-molecular-simulation-group/HierGO) explicitly includes non-epoxide ether in `--aged` recipes, and its charge table includes ETH. That demonstrates implementation coverage, not experimental proof of a universal motif ratio. The archived [GAFF2–AIM source models](https://github.com/arvpinto/GO_sheets) cover basal hydroxyl/epoxy GO and supply no automatic ether/lactone extension. Any additional block needs disjoint atom ownership, charge/protonation constraints, graph-specific typing, QM and held-out validation. No model or library was changed for this literature review.
